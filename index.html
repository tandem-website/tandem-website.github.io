TANDEM: Task and Motion Planning with As‑Needed Demonstrations for Efficient Vision‑Language‑Action Model Fine‑tuning

Anonymous Authors
Paper under review
Paper · coming soon Code · coming soon Appendix

Abstract

Human teleoperators spend substantial time demonstrating behaviors that robots can already perform autonomously, limiting the scalability of data collection for robot foundation models. Task and motion planning (TAMP) can automate many of these behaviors, but a fixed planning domain may not support every stage of a long-horizon manipulation task.

We present TANDEM (Tamp with As-Needed Demonstrations for Efficient Model fine-tuning), a system that combines TAMP with selective human teleoperation to collect demonstrations for tasks beyond the planner's capabilities. Our key idea is to represent human assistance as an on-demand planning capability. Given a language instruction and visual observation, TANDEM uses pretrained vision-language models to extend the planning domain with missing predicates and human-executed magic operators. This allows the planner to interleave autonomous and human-executed stages without task-specific intervention points. After each human stage, TANDEM re-perceives the scene and checks whether the intended effects hold before resuming autonomous planning. To support fine-tuning vision-language-action (VLA) models, TANDEM also uses example pretraining trajectories to align planner-generated motions with the target model's pretraining distribution.

We evaluate TANDEM on five long-horizon manipulation tasks beyond the TAMP domain's capabilities. On a representative long-horizon task, TANDEM collects 2.9× as many demonstrations as full-task teleoperation at the same human intervention time. Fine-tuning a pretrained π0.5-DROID model on 20 TANDEM demonstrations per task increases average task success from 0% to 60% across the five tasks.

Method

Given a language instruction and an initial observation, TANDEM uses a VLM to extend its base TAMP domain. When the base controllers cannot achieve a desired state, the VLM jointly generates magic operators and any new predicates needed to express their preconditions and effects. The extended domain supports task planning with both autonomous and human execution.

1 · Predicates

Predicate Invention

The generated predicates extend the base vocabulary. Each has a name and a natural-language definition with arguments. For example, IsOpen(x) is defined as “x is open”. The natural-language definition also specifies what to check during verification: for each predicate, a VLM-based classifier evaluates the instantiated description against the image.

2 · Operators

Magic Operators

The generated magic operators specify transitions executed through human teleoperation. Each has the symbolic structure of an ordinary operator, with a controller that invokes human teleoperation. Its preconditions and effects may use both base and invented predicates. The task planner uses these symbolic transitions to compose a plan. LiftOpen(box) has the effect IsOpen(box), so the planner can open the box before placing the bread inside.

3 · Execution

Task Plan Generation and Execution

A VLM plans over the extended domain, specifying the subgoal and executor of each phase. For each robot phase, the underlying TAMP system performs its own task and motion planning. For each human phase, the operator takes control of the robot through the teleoperation interface. After every phase, TANDEM acquires a fresh image. After a human phase, the VLM evaluates the intended effects using the predicate classifiers. If verification fails, TANDEM terminates the trial and excludes its trajectory from the dataset.

4 · Alignment

Demonstration Generation

We record the observation–action trajectories from all phases of a successful trial as one complete demonstration. Planner-generated motions may differ from the teleoperated pretraining data of the target VLA, limiting their usefulness for fine-tuning. To align TAMP-generated motions with human teleoperation, we use example pretraining trajectories to guide autonomous motion generation with DATAFARM. The human-executed portions are already collected through teleoperation and need no separate alignment step.

Tasks

We evaluate TANDEM on five long-horizon manipulation tasks that combine autonomous pick-and-place behaviors with manipulation stages outside the capabilities of the underlying TAMP system (e.g., contact-rich manipulation). Each task therefore requires TANDEM to combine autonomous execution with at least one human-assisted stage.

  • Cover Bread RollsRequires placing three breads on a plate and subsequently covering them with a cloth.“place the 3 breads on the plate and then cover the plate with the cloth”TAMP→Teleop
  • Solve Constrained PuzzleRequires removing an obstructing toy before solving a puzzle.“place the toy on the cloth and solve the puzzle”TAMP→Teleop
  • Sort & Cover SnacksRequires semantic object sorting followed by covering the bread bowl with a cloth.“first, place the bread in the blue bowl. second, place the fruit in the green bowl and cover the blue bowl with the cloth”TAMP→Teleop
  • Open Obstructed BookRequires removing an obstruction before opening a book.“take the marker off the book and place it in the wooden tray, then open the book”TAMP→Teleop
  • Store Bread in Closed BoxRequires moving the bread off the lid before opening the box and placing the bread inside.“place the bread on the plate, and then open the box and place the bread in the box”TAMP→Teleop→TAMP

Results

For each task, we collect 20 successful TANDEM demonstrations and fine-tune a separate π0.5-DROID on them, then run 20 evaluation trials per approach per task. HITL-TAMP is the closest prior approach: demonstrations of human-executed segments train a local diffusion policy, which is then combined with TAMP for execution.

Human effort and demonstration efficiency

We compare TANDEM with full-task human teleoperation while matching the total human intervention time available to both methods on Cover Bread Rolls. Because TANDEM delegates routine pick-and-place stages to TAMP, the operator only controls the robot during the unsupported covering stage. In contrast, Human teleop (oracle) requires the operator to execute every stage of every demonstration.

Scaling with matched human effort on Cover Bread Rolls

Human effort goes where TAMP cannot. The key advantage is not that TANDEM eliminates human effort, but that it concentrates human effort on the capabilities that TAMP cannot provide, allowing the same human-time budget to generate substantially more demonstrations.
More demonstrations, better policies. Success increases from 45% with 20 demonstrations to 75% with 40 and 80% with 60. With 80 demonstrations, success decreases slightly to 75%, suggesting that performance begins to saturate at approximately 60 demonstrations in this experiment.

Automatic extension of an incomplete TAMP domain

Raw TAMP cannot complete any of the five tasks because each contains at least one manipulation stage that lies outside the capabilities represented by its pick-and-place domain. TANDEM automatically identifies these unsupported stages, constructs the corresponding human-executed phases, and interleaves them with autonomous TAMP execution. Across the five tasks, TANDEM successfully collected 100 demonstrations from 130 execution attempts, a 76.9% demonstration collection success rate.

Demonstration collection attempts by outcome

The remaining failures are primarily caused by the underlying TAMP system rather than TANDEM's invention of predicates and magic operators. 93.3% of failed episodes are attributed to TAMP execution and none to TAMP planning or the human operator, while 6.7% stem from predicate/operator invention or phase switching, all of which occur on Store Bread in Closed Box.
